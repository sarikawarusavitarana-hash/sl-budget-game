Frequently Asked Questions

Sri Lanka Budget Game (Educational Simulation)

What is the Budget Game?

The Budget Game is an interactive simulation that lets you make decisions about Sri Lanka’s national Budget. You will make choices on revenue, education, health, welfare, energy, infrastructure, agriculture, disaster management and other areas of government spending. Your decisions affect the Budget’s deficit, debt, economic growth, inflation and market confidence. The game is designed to help you understand the trade-offs involved in making a national Budget.

Is this a real government Budget?

No. The Budget Game is an educational simulation. It uses real Sri Lankan economic and Budget data as its starting point, but the effects of your choices are game assumptions, not official government forecasts. For example, if an option says that spending changes by +4%, that does not mean the Government has proposed a 4% increase. It is a simulation value used to show the direction and scale of a choice.

Why are some numbers labelled as “simulation assumptions”?

The real-world effect of a Budget decision cannot be reduced to a single precise percentage. For example, increasing infrastructure spending does not automatically increase GDP by a fixed amount. The game therefore uses simplified assumptions to demonstrate relationships and trade-offs.

These assumptions are designed to help answer questions such as:

  • What happens if I spend more?
  • What happens if I cut spending?
  • What happens if I raise taxes?
  • What happens if I prioritise investment over short-term savings?

They are not intended to predict exactly what would happen in the real economy.

What is the main lesson of the Budget Game?

There is no Budget in which everything can increase at the same time. Government has limited resources, so every choice involves trade-offs.

Raising more revenue can affect households and businesses. Cutting spending can affect public services. Increasing investment can support growth but may require more financing. Subsidies can protect households while increasing fiscal costs. Investing in climate resilience can require spending today to reduce future losses.

The challenge is not simply to spend more or spend less. It is to decide what Sri Lanka should prioritise and understand the consequences of those choices.

Why do some choices increase spending while others reduce it?

Each choice represents a different policy direction.

A decision to expand welfare, increase infrastructure investment or provide broader energy subsidies may require additional government spending. A decision to reduce coverage, target subsidies or prioritise maintenance over new projects may reduce spending.

The percentages are used to make these choices visible within the simulation. They are game assumptions, not forecasts of the actual size of a future Budget decision.

Can I choose the “correct” Budget?

There is no single correct Budget. Different Budgets can prioritise different objectives.

One player may prioritise fiscal discipline and debt reduction. Another may put greater weight on social protection or public investment. The purpose of the game is to show what happens when those priorities interact with one another.

The Budget produced by the game is a simulation outcome. It is not intended to represent, predict or reproduce the actual Budget that the Government will present.

What happens if I miss a target?

The game does not simply give you a pass or fail. Your final results show how your Budget performs against several fiscal indicators.

You may meet some targets while missing others. This is deliberate: real Budgets often involve competing objectives and difficult choices.
